## 1. Problem

**Who is affected?**

Individuals and small organizations responsible for maintaining multiple vehicles or pieces of equipment, particularly those without a dedicated maintenance management system.

**What's wrong today?**

Maintenance records are often scattered across paper inspection forms, spreadsheets, and individual service logs. This makes it difficult to track completed inspections, identify unresolved deficiencies, and determine whether vehicles or equipment are ready for use.

**How will we know it worked?**

During prototype testing, at least 80% of representative users should be able to register an asset, complete a preventive maintenance inspection, and identify an unresolved deficiency without assistance.

## 2. Proposed Solution

FieldWorks is a web-based preventive maintenance management application designed to centralize vehicle and equipment records, routine inspections, deficiency reporting, and operational readiness tracking. The application will help users document maintenance conditions, identify equipment requiring attention, and maintain organized inspection records without the complexity of a full enterprise maintenance platform.

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | Description | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Do nothing | Continue using paper forms, spreadsheets, and informal maintenance records. | No additional development or implementation costs. | Records remain fragmented, deficiencies may be overlooked, and readiness is difficult to assess. |
| Option A — Existing commercial software | Adopt an established maintenance management application. | Existing functionality, vendor support, and potentially faster deployment. | Subscription costs, unnecessary features, and limited customization for smaller operations. |
| **Option B — FieldWorks (Recommended)** | Develop a focused web application for vehicle and equipment maintenance tracking. | Tailored workflows, manageable scope, and centralized inspection records. | Requires development time, testing, and ongoing maintenance. |

## 4. Can We, and Is It Worth It?

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Will people actually use it?** | The application addresses a recognizable maintenance-tracking problem. Actual demand and usability will be evaluated through user interviews and prototype testing. |
| **Can we build it with what we have?** | Yes, provided the initial scope remains limited to asset registration, preventive maintenance inspections, deficiency tracking, and readiness reporting. The three-person team can use available course resources and development tools. |
| **Can it be done in time to matter?** | Yes, if essential workflows are prioritized and additional features are deferred. The course schedule provides opportunities for requirements development, design, implementation, and testing. |
| **What does it cost?** | The academic prototype is expected to have approximately $0 in direct software and hosting expenses using free development tools and GitHub Pages. Annual hosting costs may remain $0 while free-tier services meet project needs. Team labor, production hosting, database services, and ongoing maintenance would require separate estimates. |
| **What do we gain?** | Centralized maintenance records, improved visibility of unresolved deficiencies, and clearer equipment readiness reporting. Prototype testing will measure whether at least 80% of representative users can complete the primary workflow without assistance. |

## 5. Recommendation

Proceed with FieldWorks as a focused web application for vehicle and equipment preventive maintenance management. Approval is requested to begin requirements development and prototype design. Starting now allows sufficient time to validate user needs, refine the system, and complete the project within the course schedule.
